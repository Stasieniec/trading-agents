Lessons (cite trades):
1. Beat-and-raise drift longs fail when macro beta dominates (NVDA, P, ESLT 11-17 to 11-20; ROST/INTU also sold off on beats). Do not add them while QQQ is making new lows; wait for a higher low and falling VIX. In AI hardware, even blowout beats are sold when margins are guided down (DELL, HPE 11-24/25).
2. Honour stops in a rout; do not override (NVDA: the stop saved ~11 more points). Size stops to the vol regime: with SPY moving 3-5%/day an 8-10% stop on a 4-6% daily-vol stock sits inside 2 sigma.
3. Gap risk: a stop is not a guarantee; NVDA filled -10.0% vs -9.1% stop, P -12.2% vs ~-12% planned.
4. Pre-market direction in this tape is noisy both ways (11-19 +2.1% pre then -2.2%; 11-25 -2.9% pre then -0.65%). Do not trade index pre-market moves. Cash is winning versus SPY, so cash needs a real edge to be replaced.
5. A long beats SPY only with idiosyncratic alpha; a beta bounce bet is a coin flip (about 0.5).
6. Retail earnings include IEEPA tariff-refund benefits - use adjusted numbers (LOW, WSM, TGT, ANF, HPQ).
7. Sympathy gap-ups are not trades. Thin pre-market prints (<1% of avg volume) are unreliable.
8. Merger arb: check the spread (ZS vs CRWD ~1.3%; INSM 2.4% for 6 months).
9. No-chasing rule scorecard: ANF 11-24 close 168.1 (+19.7%) vs pre +10.9%, then 11-25 close 176.96 (+5.3%); DKS 153.2 then 158.6 (+3.5%). So retail beat/raise names kept drifting up for 2 days (haven vs AI tech). URBN shows soft guides are punished hard (-12%). HPQ +11.5% on 11-25 after a pre-market read of "mixed". Lesson: when a beat has a clean guide, strength continues; the no-chasing rule is too strict for non-AI consumer/hardware names.
